# Governance: community-shaped by design

Kurmi is an open project. No contributor has permanent ownership of the idea or unilateral authority over the architecture merely by having started work. Influence should follow transparent participation, demonstrated care, and accountable review.

## Decision levels
1. Routine, reversible changes: maintainers may merge after review and CI.
2. Cross-cutting design changes: open a proposal with context, alternatives, migration impact, security/privacy implications, and a review window.
3. High-impact or hard-to-reverse changes: require explicit community discussion, a recorded decision, and a rollback plan.
4. Emergency security response: maintainers may temporarily restrict details or revert a dangerous change, then publish an explanation when safe.

## Proposal lifecycle
Draft, open discussion, revise, review, decision record, implementation, follow-up. A prototype is not automatically an approved design.

## Review expectations
- Seek review from affected contributors and relevant domain experts.
- Explain disagreement and record minority concerns where useful.
- Prefer evidence, small experiments, and reversible interfaces over authority claims.
- Disclose conflicts of interest.
- Avoid merging high-impact changes without independent review.

## Maintainers
Maintainer status is a responsibility, not ownership. Roles, permissions, and succession should be documented publicly. Access follows least privilege and is reviewed periodically. GitHub organization membership and platform achievements depend on actual permissions and GitHub rules; they cannot be guaranteed by this project.

## Decision records
Use docs/DECISIONS/ for consequential decisions. Include status, context, alternatives, decision, consequences, dissent, and revisit conditions.

Governance itself can be proposed for change. Open an issue, explain who benefits or bears risk, allow reasonable discussion, and document the outcome.
